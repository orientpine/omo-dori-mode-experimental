# Rollover: moving the lead or a lane to a new session

A session that runs for a long time grows its context, compacts it again and again, and can get worse at its work, or get stuck repeating one failed fix. A rollover moves the work to a fresh session: `dori handover` for the lead, `dori handover --lane <key>` for a lane. The owner decides each rollover. Dori only measures, proposes and logs, until the owner turns the automation on.

## The rule: a rollover needs the owner's approval

- `dori handover` and `dori handover --lane <key>` refuse a real run without `--approved-by <ref>` (`--dry-run` needs none). The ref is either a question card `Qn` that the owner answered with its first option (the card must exist and be answered, and the answer must be that option), a link to the owner's message, or their words (3 characters or more). The ref goes into the note, the lane history and the rollover log.
- `auto:<key>` is accepted only while `handover.autoRolloverLanes` (for lanes) or `handover.autoRolloverLead` (for the lead) is on. Both are off by default. Turn one on only after the owner looked at `dori rollover-stats` and said so.
- Never move a real lane or the lead without that approval, not even to test. Tests run on a scratch lane (`dori launch` one, `dori pause` it so freshness leaves it alone, close it afterwards).

## The flow

1. `dori freshness` reads each working lane's and the lead's signals: context use from the status line, compactions and age from the session log, and for lanes the attempts of one same fix in the current session (`repeats`, counted as `dori fix-attempt` counts them). When one crosses its threshold, it prints `ROLLOVER_DUE <key|lead> <what crossed> in <pane>`, once per pane. Paused lanes are skipped.
2. The lead runs `dori rollover-propose <key|lead>`. It runs a dry-run handover (the note the new session would get, and what blocks the move now) and posts a question card (`handover.proposalText`, options `handover.proposalOptions`; the first means yes) in the lane's work thread, or in the channel for the lead. The proposal goes into `<stateDir>/rollover-log.jsonl`. `--dry-run` prints the card without posting it.
3. When the owner answers yes: for a lane, ask it to reach a safe point first (`[LEAD] reach a safe point, then run dori handover ready <key> --note <file with what you were doing>`), then run `dori handover --lane <key> --approved-by Qn`. For the lead, `dori handover --approved-by Qn --note <file>`.
4. `dori rollover-stats` sums the log up: signal values at each proposal, the time to approval, each step's time and result, and each lane's watch-log quality signals (NUDGED, NO-REPORT, SAME_FIX_3, STUCK, INTENT_DRIFT, NOT_SUBMITTED, LANE_BLOCKED) in the 24 hours before and after (`--window H`). The automation thresholds are set from this.

## When a session counts as long

Measured on 2026-10-11 over 183 local session logs (27,652 assistant turns): 15 lead sessions and 75 registered lane sessions, against the quality labels the records hold (owner corrections in lead sessions, `[LEAD] not done` and other rejections in lanes, done objections, tool errors, SAME_FIX / INTENT_DRIFT / NUDGED lines in the watch log, and errors in the 10 turns after each compaction compared with the 10 before).

**What the data shows: no length at which quality measurably drops.**

- Lanes: all 7 rejections came in the first 6 hours and before any compaction. Error rates do not rise with context use, compactions, age or turns. The longest lane session (E7: 3,777 turns, 45.8 h, 12 compactions) has no rejection.
- Lead: 2 owner corrections in total, both in one short session (turns 72 and 103). The longest lead session (3,888 turns, 85 h, 7 compactions) has none.
- Around compactions: 0 corrections in the 10 turns before or after any of 34 compactions; 1 tool error after, 0 before.
- Every candidate cutoff (context 40/60/80 %, 1/2/4 compactions, 6/24/48 h) fires mostly on sessions that later show no problem: 71-100 % of its alerts. The labels are few, so this does not prove long sessions are safe either.

For comparison, these length cutoffs would have asked only the longest sessions, about the top 5 % in the measured logs. They are not the defaults; see the decision below.

| Side | Context use | Compactions | Age | Sessions that would have been asked |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lane | >= 80 % | >= 4 | >= 24 h | 6 by context, 4 by compactions, 4 by age, of 75 |
| Lead | >= 80 % | >= 4 | >= 48 h | 2 by context, 1 by compactions, 3 by age, of 15 |

### What published work shows

Searched on 2026-10-11 by eight research lanes (benchmarks, vendor docs, tool defaults, compaction studies, long-horizon studies, loop detection, practitioner reports, and one lane that only attacked the claims), about 150 searches. Every source below was read, not taken from a search snippet; no browser was attached, so pages that need one (Fiction.LiveBench, Reddit) are marked secondary or left out. No source measures a rollover threshold for claude-opus-5-5, or compares continuing a coding session with a fresh one at matched state.

| Candidate signal | Evidence | Rating | Setting |
|---|---|---|---|
| Context use (%) | Benchmarks show real loss with length, but where it starts depends on model, thinking mode and task; vendor and tool triggers are overflow and cost reserves, not quality points; frontier 1M models differ widely; the 40-60 % rule is one practitioner's workflow | weak (근거 약함) | 0 (off) |
| Compactions | Repeated compression loses rules in a narrow lab setup and the first action after a compaction errs more; coding-task studies find no significant solve-rate drop from summaries; vendors call repeated compaction safe; the 2-3 count comes from user reports | weak (근거 약함) | 0 (off) |
| Session age (h) | Nothing isolates elapsed time; drift follows the history's content, not its length or age; the best models ran a simulated year without degradation | none (근거 없음) | 0 (off) |
| Same failed fix repeated | Vendor guidance (reset after two failed corrections), three tools' default limit of 3, and a controlled study where a fresh restart after about the 3rd failed debugging attempt helped every model tested; in-session correction also works, so it is a point to offer a reset, not a rule to force one | strong as a heuristic (근거 있음) | lanes: 3 |

The sources, with their conditions and numbers:

- **Length.** NoLiMa ([arxiv 2502.05167](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.05167v1), 2025-02, latent-association retrieval 1-32K): effective length 8K for GPT-4o, 4K for Claude 3.5 Sonnet (85.4 -> 29.8 %), 2K for Gemini 1.5 Pro, while literal matching stays at 98 %. Chroma Context Rot ([trychroma.com](https://www.trychroma.com/research/context-rot), 2025-07, LongMemEval, ~300 tokens vs ~113K with the needed facts kept): Opus 4 92 -> 38 % without thinking but 95 -> 72 % with it; Gemini 2.5 Pro thinking 96 -> 90 %. GPT-5.4 ([openai.com](https://openai.com/index/introducing-gpt-5-4/), 2026-03, MRCR v2 8-needle): 86.0 % at 64-128K, 79.3 % at 128-256K, 57.5 % at 256-512K, 36.6 % at 512K-1M. Opus 4.6 on the same test: 76 % at 1M ([anthropic.com](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-opus-4-6), 2026-02), 78.3 % at GA ([claude.com](https://claude.com/blog/1m-context-ga), 2026-03); Gemini 3.1 Pro 26.3 % at 1M ([model card](https://deepmind.google/models/model-cards/gemini-3-1-pro/)). Coding: LongCodeBench ([arxiv 2505.07897](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.07897v1)) Claude 3.5 Sonnet issue fixes 29 % at 32K -> 3 % at 256K, Gemini 2.5 Pro 24 % at 256K -> 7 % at 1M, but code QA flat (GPT-4.1 72.6 -> 80.0 %). Counter: Michelangelo ([arxiv 2409.12640](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.12640v1)) finds no further loss from 128K to 1M after an early drop; HELMET ([arxiv 2410.02694](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.02694v1)) finds the loss task-dependent (reranking falls, RAG and citation stay flat) and needle tests correlate at most .8 with real tasks. And cutting context can hurt more than length: removing 75 % of a BABILong context naively costs Opus 4.7 .433, while removing only distractors gains .008 ([arxiv 2608.03297](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.03297v1), 2026-08).
- **Vendor and tool triggers.** Claude Code compacts native 1M models at about 967K tokens ([model-config](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/model-config#default-auto-compact-thresholds)); Codex at 90 % of the window (244,800 of 272,000; [source @9f3d255](https://github.com/openai/codex/blob/9f3d25588b3f0bdf452d4eda919f2c2365a89bba/codex-rs/protocol/src/openai_models.rs#L460-L537)); Cline 0.9 of usable input; OpenCode 10 % buffer; Amp 90 %; Goose min(80 %, 225K), capped below a price tier; Gemini CLI 50 % (20 % in older versions); Continue 121K of 200K. All of them are about overflow or cost, and they range from 50 to 97 %. Anthropic's API compaction trigger defaults to 150,000 tokens ([docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/compaction-threshold)). OpenAI says compaction is "safe to run repeatedly in long sessions" ([GPT-5.2 guide](https://developers.openai.com/cookbook/examples/gpt-5/gpt-5-2_prompting_guide)). Anthropic dropped context resets entirely once Opus 4.5 arrived and ran one continuous session with compaction; resets had been essential for Sonnet 4.5 ([harness design, 2026-03-24](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/harness-design-long-running-apps)).
- **Compaction quality.** Compaction Cliff ([arxiv 2608.22752](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.22752), 2026-08): Sonnet 4.6 with the Claude Code compact prompt keeps .53 of a configuration's rules after one round and .10 after five, at 50 % budget each round (3 % of the original at the end), with no new work in between; pinning the rules keeps .96. TRACE ([arxiv 2608.06503](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.06503v1), 2026-08, AppWorld, 2-16K budgets): the first action after a compaction is blocked or wrong +0.108 more often. Counter, on real coding: SWE-agent on SWE-bench Verified with summaries or masking, up to 250 turns, shows no significant solve-rate loss (Qwen3-Coder 54.8 vs 53.8 %; [arxiv 2508.21433](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.21433v2)); OpenHands' condenser solves 54 % vs 53 % without it ([blog, 2025-04](https://hub.openhands.dev/blog/openhands-context-condensensation-for-more-efficient-ai-agents)); in a harness study the gain from context management shrinks from 35.7 to 2.7 points as the window grows from 32K to 128K, so it mostly prevents overflow ([arxiv 2609.20804](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.20804)). The count rules ("0-2 compactions excellent, 9+ degraded", [claude-code #33212](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-code/issues/33212); "forgets the plan after 2") are single-user reports. A fresh session built from the same lossy summary inherits the same gaps, so what the note carries matters more than the count.
- **Age and many turns.** Vending-Bench ([arxiv 2502.15840](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.15840v1)): days until sales stop correlate r = 0.167 with days until the memory fills; Vending-Bench 2 ([andonlabs.com](https://andonlabs.com/evals/vending-bench-2), 2025-11) reports the best models running a simulated year "with no signs of performance degradation". Goal Drift ([arxiv 2505.02709](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.02709v1)): replacing the history with length-matched filler removes most drift, so the history's pattern drives it, not its length. Multi-turn underspecification costs 39 % on average already from two turns ([arxiv 2505.06120](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.06120v1)), which is about anchoring on early wrong answers, not age. METR: "Time horizon is not the length of time AIs can work independently" ([note, 2026-01](https://metr.org/notes/2026-01-22-time-horizon-limitations/)).
- **Repeated failed fixes.** Claude Code: "After two failed corrections, `/clear` and write a better initial prompt" ([best practices](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/best-practices)). Cline and Roo default to 3 consecutive mistakes; OpenHands nudges at the 3rd identical action and error and stops at the 4th; Gemini CLI nudges once on a loop, then aborts. Debugging decay ([Scientific Reports 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-27846-5), 18 models, HumanEval): fixing ability mostly collapses after the 3rd attempt, and restarting fresh at the same attempt budget improved all 6 models tested (e.g. 72.6 -> 82.8 %). Models condition on their own earlier errors beyond any length effect, though thinking models resisted it ([arxiv 2509.09677](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.09677)). Counter: in a production A/B test, guidance appended to the running session also cut failures ([arxiv 2602.17037](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.17037v2)). And the new session should get what the failed attempts ruled out, not the failed command itself: replaying it verbatim makes repeats more likely ([arxiv 2608.23651](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23651)).

### The decision

- Lanes: `handover.laneRepeats` = 3. On the 3rd attempt of one same fix in the current session (the existing `SAME_FIX_3` point), freshness says the rollover is due, and the lead may propose it. The owner still decides; often the better answer is a changed hypothesis in the same session (`dori fix-attempt` asks for three).
- Context use, compactions and age: 0 (off) for lanes and the lead. The evidence does not give a number that transfers to claude-opus-5-5 at 1M, and the local logs show no onset. `dori rollover-propose` still works by hand at any time, for example when the owner sees a session go wrong, and it logs the signal values either way.
- The handover note matters more than the timing: it carries the brief, the owner's goal, todos, the ready note (what was tried and ruled out) and the monitors, so the new session does not start from a lossy summary.
- Re-decide from `dori rollover-stats` after the first rollovers: the quality lines in the 24 hours before and after each one, and the owner's yes/no on the proposals. The missing evidence is a matched comparison on this model: the same checkpoint continued vs moved to a fresh session. The automation keys stay off until then.

## What a lane handover carries and what it refuses

The note (`<stateDir>/handover/lanes/<key>-<stamp>.md`, plus `.json`) holds: the brief path, owner goal and Done, open todos and goal of the old session, every monitor it runs (persistent or not; the new session re-arms each as persistent), its local chains, its detached jobs, its last report, direction and history, the work thread, model and thinking level, the lane's own notes (`dori handover ready --note`) and the lead's (`--note`), and the approval ref.

- **Local chain**: a child process of the lane's omo agent that is neither omo's own helper (MCP servers, the subagent RPC host, the provider CLI) nor a monitor. It ends when the old session closes. The handover refuses while one runs, unless the lane listed it in `dori handover ready` (run in its own pane, valid `handover.readyMin` minutes, covering only the pids it listed then) or `--force` is given.
- **Detached job**: a process outside the pane's process tree whose environment still names the lane's omo session (`PI_SESSION_ID`) or aoe session. It survives, is listed, and is never touched. `HANDOVER_DETACHED_ALIVE n/m` after the close says how many still run.
- omo's python eval kernel counts as a local chain too: a cell running in it (detached cells keep running while the agent is idle) would end with the session, so the lane has to list it with `dori handover ready`.
- A running turn (`esc to interrupt`) or an open question in the pane also refuses, unless the lane marked a safe point.
- It refuses when run from the lane's own pane: the lead runs it.

## Failure modes

Each row is a point where a rollover can break, what is left, how it is put right, and the test that holds it (`scripts/test/lane-handover.test.ts` = L, `scripts/test/handover.test.ts` = H).

| Where it breaks | State left | How it is put right | Test |
|---|---|---|---|
| Before anything opens: no approval, an unanswered or "no" card, a live chain, a running turn, an open question, run from the lane's pane, no live aoe session | nothing changed | `HANDOVER_REFUSED`, exit 2; a refused real attempt is logged | L "refuses while a local chain...", L "refuses without the owner's approval", L "an approval is...", H "refuses without the owner's approval" |
| `aoe add` fails | nothing changed | `HANDOVER_FAIL`, exit 3 | (aoe error path, same as the lead's) |
| The new session does not start: usage limit or quota exhausted, crash, no prompt in 3 min | a new session that is not working | it is stopped and trashed, the old one is told it stays, exit 3 | L "a new session that does not start is closed" |
| The first message does not register | same | same | (sendVerified path) |
| The new session runs at another thinking level or model (omo can start at medium) | lane already pointed at the new pane | lane and cards back to the old pane, the new session closed, the old told, exit 3 | L "rolls back when the new session runs at another thinking level" |
| A monitor is not re-armed in time | same | same | L "rolls back when a monitor is not re-armed..." |
| The probe is not acked, or acked from the old pane | same | same | L same test (acked from another pane) |
| An exception in the middle (a disk, a tool or a parse error) | lane may point at either pane | caught: the same repair as a killed run | L "an error in the middle of the move is repaired" |
| The handover process is killed or the host reboots midway | `<stateDir>/handover/lanes/<key>.pending.json` left behind; both sessions may live | the next `dori handover --lane <key>` repairs first (`HANDOVER_INTERRUPTED`, `HANDOVER_RECOVERED`): old session alive -> lane back on it, new one closed; only the new one alive -> lane stays on it; neither alive (reboot) -> registry left for `dori sync` / dead-panes. A journal whose process still lives refuses a second run | L "a run killed midway...", L "a killed run whose old session is already gone..." |
| Two handovers of one lane, or of the lead, at once | - | a lock per lane (`<key>.lock`) and one for the lead; a lock whose process is gone is taken over | H "refuses while another handover holds the lock" |
| Both sessions alive during the checks: double replies | the old session could still answer the owner | the old one is told to stop, and a quiet mark makes `dori send`, `dori ask` and `dori thread` refuse from its pane (`HANDOVER_QUIET`, exit 4) until the run ends. The mark expires `verifyMin + 10` minutes after it was set, so a crashed run cannot silence a pane for good. With `--keep-old` it stays until it expires | L "the old lane is quiet while it is replaced...", H "the old lead is quiet from the moment the new lead takes over..." |
| A report arrives twice (old and new session) during the checks | two `[REPORT]` lines for one lane | harmless: freshness keeps the latest report per lane; the old one is told to stop first | (by design) |
| Closing the old session fails | the lane points at the new pane, the old idle | `HANDOVER_CLOSE_FAIL`, exit 1; close it with `aoe rm` | (closeAoeSession path) |
| An owner message arrives during the switch | the listener routes by the registry | before the repoint (seconds) it reaches the old pane, which keeps it if the handover rolls back; after it, the new pane | (by design) |
| A ready mark older than `readyMin`, or from another pane | - | ignored (`HANDOVER_READY_STALE`) | L "a ready mark covers only..." |
| The lane marks ready from omo's eval kernel, which stays alive while it idles | the kernel would count as a live chain and block the move | the ready mark also covers the process that ran `dori handover ready` | L "dori handover ready runs only in the lane's own pane and also covers..." |
| The new session starts a local chain again while the old copy still runs (the first live try did this) | the same work twice | the note says not to start them; after the close the handover checks the pids: all ended -> `[LEAD] HANDOVER_CHAINS_ENDED` (restart from checkpoints, skip finished steps); one still alive -> `HANDOVER_CHAIN_SURVIVED` (do not restart). Live re-check: never two copies in 84 samples | L "moves the lane..." (no double run), L "a chain that outlives the close is not restarted..." |
| The account pool gives the new session another account | - | `HANDOVER_ACCOUNT` line; the rollover log keeps both accounts | L "a chain that outlives the close... and a new account is logged" |
